# Voice Rules — What Works, What Doesn't

> The voice is the product. When copy sounds like every other AI-generated "AI tool," the buyer tunes out. When it sounds like a real founder who's already done the work, the buyer leans in. This file is the rulebook for the second kind.

---

## The Core Frame

**Remove the AI from marketing. Sell the relief of the result.**

The buyer doesn't want to know that you used AI to build your product. The buyer wants to know that the product saves them time, makes them money, or removes a specific pain. The moment "AI" enters the marketing copy, the buyer's brain switches to anxiety mode: "Will this replace me? Is this a tool or a service? Is this person real?"

The right frame: **the buyer gets the result.** How you produced it is irrelevant. Sell the relief, not the production method.

---

## Banned Words and Phrases (Marketing Copy)

These are the words that signal "AI-generated marketing copy." Don't use them in any copy targeting a buyer, prospect, or external audience.

### Banned in any external copy:

- "AI-powered," "AI does the work," "leveraging AI," "AI workflow"
- "Automation" (triggers replacement anxiety)
- "Smart," "intelligent," "advanced," "cutting-edge," "next-gen"
- "In today's rapidly evolving landscape" (or any other AI-ism)
- "Robust," "seamless," "streamline," "leverage"
- "Unlock," "unleash," "supercharge," "crushing it"
- "Game-changer," "revolutionary," "disruptive"
- Em-dashes in punchy copy (use periods, colons, or whitespace)
- Emojis in B2B body copy (Gumroad and Reddit punish them)

### Banned in startup/founder pitch context:

- "10x," "hustle," "synergies"
- "Move fast and break things"
- "We are passionate about" / "we love what we do" / "world-class team"

## Required Patterns (Marketing Copy)

### 1. Drop-in framing

The product is a "drop-in" or "ready-to-deploy" or "pre-cooked." The buyer doesn't have to learn, configure, or set it up. They get the filet on a silver platter, not the fishing lesson.

**Instead of:** "Our system helps you build proposals faster."
**Use:** "Paste a 45-minute discovery call transcript in. Get a $15k proposal back, ready to send."

### 2. Relief framing

Lead with what the buyer GETS, not what the tool DOES. The tool is the means. The result is the sell.

**Instead of:** "Our AI generates proposals."
**Use:** "Look like you spent 4 hours on this proposal. (You spent 8 minutes.)"

### 3. Schadenfreude framing (where appropriate)

For some audiences, the sell is the guilty pleasure of unearned efficiency. "You didn't work harder. You worked smarter" — but stated more sharply. The buyer feels like they got away with something.

**Use when:** the audience is founders, solo operators, agency owners, freelancers — people who feel the grind and want a shortcut that doesn't feel like cheating.

**Don't use when:** the audience is enterprise, regulated industries, or contexts where legitimacy matters more than efficiency.

### 4. Numbers always

Every claim gets a number. Every number gets context.

**Instead of:** "Increased leads significantly."
**Use:** "Increased MQLs by 287% — equivalent to $340k in additional pipeline based on their close rate × deal size."

### 5. The exact guarantee (when applicable)

When a product has a risk reversal, use a specific, time-bound, outcome-based guarantee. The structure is:

> "If [specific outcome doesn't happen] within [specific time period], [specific refund action]. No [friction words]."

**Example:** "If it doesn't save you at least 3 hours of grueling work the very first time you use it, I will refund your $37 in full and you keep everything."

## The Voice: American B2B English, No Fluff

Target register: American small-business owner, 30-50, sharp, skeptical of marketing speak, reads copy in 5 seconds and decides.

### Tonal rules:

- **Short sentences.** Punchy. Each paragraph does work, not decoration.
- **Specific vocabulary.** Use the buyer's industry terms. "MQLs," "ARR," "NPS" — whatever they use. Match their world.
- **First person, present tense.** "I went from 3 hours to 8 minutes" not "One could potentially reduce the time required."
- **No hedging.** "Saves 4-6 hours/week" not "may potentially help reduce the time spent on."
- **No "we."** The product is the subject, not the company. "The system generates..." not "We generate..."
- **No corporate transitions.** "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally" — out.

### Banned sentence structures:

- "It's important to note that..."
- "In conclusion..."
- "Whether you're a [X] or a [Y]..."
- "At the end of the day..."
- "Let's dive in..."
- "Without further ado..."

## Voice Calibration by Context

| Context | Voice |
|---|---|
| **Gumroad listing description** | Burner-asset, schadenfreude, numbers, drop-in |
| **Reddit post** | Conversational, story-first, no marketing speak, first-person, self-deprecating where it fits |
| **Twitter thread** | Hook-heavy, 1-line insight per tweet, soft CTA at the end |
| **Email to prospect** | Direct, callback to specific detail from the call, single CTA |
| **Sales call** | Mirror the buyer's language, ask questions, slow down when they slow down |
| **Slack/Discord to peers** | Casual, no caps, no emojis unless they used them first |
| **Internal docs (for Gabriel)** | Blunt, structured, decisions and risks first, no preamble |

## The Test

Before publishing any copy, run the "AI tells" check. Search the text for:

1. Banned words (above)
2. Em-dashes in punchy prose
3. Emojis in B2B context
4. "It's important to note that" / "Let's dive in" / "At the end of the day"
5. "We" / "our" / "us" in product-describing copy
6. Hedging words ("may," "might," "could," "potentially")
7. Corporate transitions ("Moreover," "Furthermore")
8. "Leverage" in any context
9. "Robust" / "seamless" / "streamline"

If any are present, fix. The fix is usually: shorter sentence, specific number, first-person present tense.
