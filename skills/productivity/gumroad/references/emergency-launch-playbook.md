## Key Lessons (from actual launches)

1. **Gumroad doesn't attract traffic** — the seller must drive ALL traffic via outreach. Zero visits = zero sales, regardless of product quality.
2. **Fake social proof doesn't work** — Gabriel's audience (tech/creator/physicist types) can smell it instantly. Never use it.
3. **Products were published at $0 initially** — verify prices are set BEFORE announcing anywhere.
4. **Browser login to Gumroad is blocked by bot detection** — page blanks when typing credentials via browser tool. User must log in on their own browser to create/edit products.
5. **Gumroad Dashboard columns**: "Sales Number" and "Revenue" are separate — don't confuse with price column. A sale at $0 (free download) shows as 1/0.
6. **The Toddler Kit is the top of funnel** — it's free ($0+), gets the attention, and should link to paid products. But even free products need active promotion to get downloads.
7. **Distribution is the only bottleneck** — the products are good, the copy is right, the pricing is fair. The missing piece is people knowing they exist.
8. **LinkedIn Premium InMail is the best channel** for Gabriel — 126 connections, 29K impressions/2 weeks, direct 1:1 reach of relevant professionals.
9. **Emergency pricing helps conversion** — under $10 is impulse territory. The Full Arsenal at $29 is a clear deal compared to $170+ individually.
10. **Code-block descriptions kill conversions** — descriptions pasted into Gumroad's editor as code (language selector shows "Arduino" or "Markdown") render as gray `<code>` boxes on the public page. They look like terminal output, not a product page. Fix: paste as plain text, reformat with the toolbar.
11. **Always audit the public page, not the editor** — the editor shows rich text but the public page may render it differently. Visit each product URL as a visitor to verify presentation.
12. **One product at a time, with explanation** — Gabriel needs to understand each finding before moving to the next. Batch work without explanation causes frustration and loss of trust.
